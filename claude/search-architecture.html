How a pasted link is searched

An architecture map of the compose form's "paste a link" flow, from the paste to the saved post: what the browser asks, which route and service answers, what is read from the database or a cache first, and when an outside service is called. Built from the code on 12 Oct 2026 (ComposeModal.jsx, routes/media.js, routes/discogs.js, routes/posts.js, services/*). The top search bar is a separate system and is not on this map.

2 · The rules that decide what happens

Matching a record (the Discogs ladder)

  • Release, shop and master links skip the search: the id is in the link (shop and master need one cached lookup).
  • Every other site goes through findDiscogsRelease: barcode first (asked of Spotify), then artist plus album, artist plus title, catalogue number; at most 6 attempts.
  • Each attempt asks our own crawled catalogue first (full-text index), and only then the Discogs search (up to 5 releases fetched in parallel).
  • A candidate only counts if title and artist both agree. With no artist, a label must agree too.
  • No Discogs match: iTunes, Deezer and MusicBrainz are asked in parallel; a candidate needs the same agreement, and can lead back to Discogs.
  • Still nothing: the blanks are filled from those catalogues and the Discogs artist and label ids are looked up, and the form says what it used.
  • Live sets and mixes skip all of this: they have no release to find.

Finding a link for a track (cheapest first)

  • 1. A link already saved for this release and position (release_track_links) wins and is never searched again.
  • 2. Discogs' own videos for the release, matched to tracks by title, checked alive (once per release per 30 days).
  • 3. The YouTube cache, then uploads from channels we have crawled (needs a good title score, and the artist for a named track).
  • 4. Free sources: Spotify, then Deezer, then Apple. A miss here is a placeholder, shown later as a 30-second preview.
  • 5. Only then a paid YouTube search, and only if the budget allows. First hit stops the search.
  • The result is saved against the release and position, so the next person who posts that record gets it free.

The YouTube budget (100 units per search)

  • Listening (a click in a spotlight or drawer) may use 70% of the day; the last 30% is kept for new posts.
  • At most 3 paid searches per release, however many people open it.
  • The release breaker: 3 misses, and more misses than three times the finds, stops paying for that release.
  • When capped or held, only the free steps run and the form says so ("daily YouTube search limit reached").
  • Posting looks for a real YouTube link first; listening looks for Spotify first. Setting YOUTUBE_FIRST=1 reverses the free-first order.

What the form does with the answer

  • The artist and label ids come back with the names and are remembered, so they survive editing the text fields.
  • A search that is superseded (a new link pasted, another release picked) is dropped, and a row you typed by hand is never overwritten.
  • Compilations search each track under its own artist, not "Various".
  • Section headings ("Phase I", catalogue-number rows) are not tracks and are never searched for.
  • The Discogs release page, not the pasted link, is what is saved: a shop listing dies once sold.

3 · What each site's reader looks at

Twelve readers, all in routes/media.js, each ending in the same shape: title, artist(s), label, catalogue number, year, cover, tracks, genres, type, and the hints used to find the Discogs release.

5 · After you press Post

Saved straight away

  • The post, its artists and labels (with Discogs ids), genres and tracks (with each track's artist and links).
  • A release that is already on the site is refused with the existing post's id, and the form offers the ♥ instead.
  • The other releases the link matched (compilations) are added to the artists' and labels' catalogues.

Done a moment later, in the background

  • About 1.5 seconds on: every link is checked, and one that cannot play (embedding off, removed) is cleared.
  • Every free place each track can play is looked up (queuePostSources).
  • The origin ("found digging", "found from", "outside link") is saved on the post, and an event goes to the activity database.

6 · Weak spots worth knowing

  • Resident Advisor and Boiler Room links only give a title guessed from the address.
  • Short or common track titles can match the wrong video; the title score threshold is the guard (and "Meftah – 7" is on the TODO).
  • Every Discogs call is rate limited; a burst of pastes can slow the Discogs step.
  • The paid YouTube search is the only step that spends quota, and the whole site shares one daily allowance.
